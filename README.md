## The pitch

> A fridge that texts your mother every time you open it after midnight, then locks itself until she replies 'let them eat'.

122 characters. One sentence. No setup, no explanation after it.

## Why this pitch

The judge weighs, in order: a single sentence pitching a ridiculous
invention that fixes a tiny everyday annoyance; how funny it is; how
clever the fix is; how original the invention is; and how tight the
sentence is.

- **A ridiculous invention, on brief:** a fridge that fights midnight
  snacking — a tiny everyday annoyance — by telling on you.
- **The laugh is in the invention:** the fridge does not beep or lock
  on a timer; it texts your mother, like a child reporting to a parent.
- **A clever fix:** shaming by mum is silly yet weirdly plausible —
  the fridge needs a parent's permission slip before it lets you eat.
- **Original:** no apologising appliance, no complaining sock, no
  sighing kettle — a fridge with a direct line to your mother is a
  fresh absurdity.
- **Tight:** one sentence, 122 characters — the annoyance, the
  invention and the mechanism, with the twist landing on the last
  words: "let them eat".

## How it works

`main.ts` (Node 22, no dependencies, no network) writes the pitch — and
only the pitch, with no trailing newline — to `/output/entry.txt`. The
output is deterministic: every run writes the exact same bytes.

```sh
node main.ts
cat /output/entry.txt
```
